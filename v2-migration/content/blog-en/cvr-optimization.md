CTR looks fine but nothing converts. People click through and then vanish at the last step. This isn't an ad problem — it's a **post-click** problem. That's why swapping creative never fixes it.

CVR (conversion rate) is conversions over visits. But the number shifts wildly depending on whether the denominator is clicks, installs, or sessions. So before diagnosing anything, get the team aligned on **what over what**. If that's off, everything below is wasted effort.

## If the ad and the landing page make different promises, people leave

This is the most common cause. The ad shouts "50% off your first order," and the landing page's first screen never mentions it. In half a second the visitor thinks "wrong place" and hits back.

This is called **message match (scent)**. The signal someone picked up in the ad has to carry through to the landing page without breaking. Three things to check:

- Does the ad headline's **key phrase** appear on the landing page's first screen?
- Do the **visuals, colors, and model** from the creative carry over?
- If you promised a discount, is that number visible **within the first scroll**?

If "what I expected is right here" doesn't land within three seconds of the click, most people bounce. Splitting landing pages per campaign and creative feels like a chore, but when the message diverges, the landing page has to diverge too — that's what keeps CVR alive.

## Count the friction, one step at a time

Every extra tap on the way to conversion leaks people. Don't eyeball it — actually count.

- **Signup form fields**: eight fields? Can you get to three? Starting with just email and password and collecting the rest later almost always wins.
- **Number of steps**: how many clicks from cart to payment? Each step you remove usually adds a few points.
- **Load speed**: on mobile, if the first screen takes over three seconds, nearly half leave before it renders. Suspect image weight first.
- **App onboarding length**: install happened, but a six-step tutorial loses people on first launch — that's an onboarding problem, not an ad problem.

Find where it leaks most with the [funnel drop-off analysis](/blog/funnel-dropoff-analysis), and fix that spot first. Touch everything at once and you'll never know what worked.

## If the wrong people showed up, fixing the landing page won't help

A low CVR can mean you brought the **wrong crowd**, not that the page is bad. Clickbait creative ("Tap now or miss out!") prints a pretty CTR but drags in uninterested clicks that crater conversion. No amount of landing-page polish rescues that — you have to look at the creative and targeting.

Quick self-check: a campaign with **above-average CTR but unusually low CVR** is almost always this. Rewrite that creative's hook from "exaggerate the benefit" to "qualify the right person." Clicks may drop, but conversions often rise.

## Is the offer itself just weak?

If the page is clean and the targeting is right and it still won't move, what's left is the **offer**. The reason to act right now is weak — a free trial shorter than competitors', a flat first-purchase incentive, no "why now." That's a product-and-pricing problem marketers often can't solve alone, but write it down anyway: **the real cause of stalled CVR is here more often than people admit.**

## When you change something, confirm it with a test

Landing A vs B, a five-field form vs three, two button labels — validate with an [A/B test](/blog/ab-testing). Change things on "this feels better" and you can't tell improvement from regression. Two rules only:

- **Wait until the sample is large enough.** "B won" off a few dozen conversions in one day is mostly noise.
- **Don't peek and stop early.** Killing a test the moment it looks good makes you see lift that isn't there (the same trap as [correlation vs causation](/blog/correlation-vs-causation)).

## Don't chase the ratio alone

One honest closing note. Chasing CVR alone often **shrinks the whole volume**. Narrow the targeting hard and CVR gets prettier while the conversion count itself collapses.

> CVR × volume = conversions

Look at the result, not the ratio. And whether those conversions actually pay off comes down to cost per conversion ([CPA](/blog/cpi-cpa-cpm-difference)) and final revenue and [LTV](/blog/ltv-cac-ratio). The ratio is a checkpoint on the way; the goal is always "conversions that make money."
