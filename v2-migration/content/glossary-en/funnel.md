## In one line

Impression → click → install → signup → purchase. That step-by-step path, where the audience shrinks at every stage, is a funnel — named for the shape it makes, wide at the top and narrowing downward.

## Why it matters

If you want to raise conversion but don't know where to start, map the funnel. The pass-through rate between each stage shows exactly where the biggest leak is.

## A common trap

The lowest pass-through stage isn't always the top priority. Weigh room for improvement × traffic volume — a stage that's already seen by a lot of users can move more total conversions with a small improvement than a low-traffic stage with a low rate.

## Run the numbers

Take a funnel of 100,000 impressions to 2,000 clicks to 800 installs to 200 signups. The step rates are 2%, 40% and 25%. Lifting the weakest of those, install to signup, from 25% to 30% gets you 240 signups — 40 more.

Now lift the very first rate instead, click-through from 2% to 2.5%. That gives 2,500 clicks, 1,000 installs and 250 signups: 50 more, without touching a single downstream rate. This is why the stage carrying the most traffic often moves more absolute conversions than the stage with the worst rate.
